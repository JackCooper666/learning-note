# i-jepa
I-JEPA 的核心目的是训练一个好的视觉表征模型。它在自监督预训练过程中，人为遮挡图像的一部分，让模型利用可见区域的信息去预测被遮挡区域的高层 representation，而不是预测具体像素。通过不断完成这种预测任务，Encoder 学会图像中的语义和空间关系。训练完成后，我们主要使用这个 pretrained Encoder 提取图像特征，再提供给分类、深度估计等下游任务。

## Pretraining Prediction Pipeline
1. Input an image and sample context and target block masks.
	- The context encoder does not see the target regions.
	- It only receives the visible context patches.
2. The context encoder generates representations of the visible context patches. $$s_{x} = f_{\theta}(x)$$ It does not generate a representation of the complete image, because the target patches have been removed.
3. The predictor predicts the representations of the patches inside each target block, using:
	- the context representation $s_{x}$, and
	- mask tokens containing the positions of the target patches $$\hat{s}_y(i) = g_\phi\left(s_x, \{m_j\}_{j \in B_i}\right)$$

2. context encoder generates the representation of the image
3. Predictor predicts the representation of patches in the target blocks accroding to the representations from the context encorder

## Training pipeline
The training pipeline mainly contents three parts:
### context encoder
input the visible patches $x$, then get the representation fo the patches $$s_{x} = f_{\theta}(x)$$the $\theta$ is the params in the context encoder needs to be trained , such as the params in the transformer of it.
### target encoder
1. The target encoder processes the complete image first. Target block representations are then selected from the output representations of the target encoder.$$s_{y} = f_{\bar \theta}(y)$$. $\bar \theta$ are the parameters of the target encoder, but they are **not directly optimized by gradient descent**.

### predictor 
1. predict the representations of target blocks according the representations from the context encoder and the position embedding. $$\hat s_{y}(i) = g_{\phi}(s_{x},\{m_{i}\}_{i\in B_{I}})$$ where $\phi$ is the params needs to be updated, $s_{x}$ is the representations of the context encoder, $m_{i}$ is mask token. mask tokens are parameterized by a **shared learnable vector with an added positional embedding**. The shared learnable vector is just placeholder.
### Loss Function

The loss function used in I-JEPA is relatively simple.

The paper uses:

$$
\mathcal{L}
=
\frac{1}{M}
\sum_{i=1}^{M}
\sum_{j \in B_i}
\left\|
\hat{s}_{y_j} - s_{y_j}
\right\|_2^2
$$

This is the **L2 loss**, also called the **squared Euclidean distance**.

#### Meaning of Each Term

#### \(M\)

$$
M = \text{number of target blocks}
$$

In the paper, the default setting is approximately:

$$
M = 4
$$

##### $(B_i)$

$(B_i)$ represents the set of patches contained in the $(i)$-th target block.

#### $(s_{y_j})$

$s_{y_j}$is the representation of the $(j)$-th target patch generated by the **target encoder**.

##### $\hat{s}_{y_j}$

$\hat{s}_{y_j}$ is the representation of the \(j\)-th target patch predicted by the **predictor**.

#### Example

Suppose the target representation is:

$$
s_{y_j} =[0.2,\ 0.5,\ -0.3]
$$

and the predicted representation is:

$$
\hat{s}_{y_j} = [0.1,\ 0.6,\ -0.1]
$$

Then the squared L2 distance is:

$$
\left\|\hat{s}_{y_j} - s_{y_j}\right\|_2^2= (0.1-0.2)^2+(0.6-0.5)^2+(-0.1+0.3)^2
$$


The training objective is:

$$
\min_{\theta,\phi} \mathcal{L}
$$

This allows the predictor to gradually learn how to infer the representations of missing target regions from the visible context.
#### Target Encoder Update Using EMA

An important mechanism in I-JEPA is that the **target encoder is not directly updated through gradient descent**.

The parameters of the **context encoder** are:

$$
\theta
$$

and they are updated using gradient-based optimization.

The parameters of the **predictor** are:

$$
\phi
$$

and they are also updated using gradient-based optimization.

However, the parameters of the **target encoder** are:

$$
\bar{\theta}
$$

and they are updated using **EMA** rather than gradient descent.

#### Exponential Moving Average

EMA stands for:

**Exponential Moving Average**

The update rule is:

$$
\bar{\theta}_t
=
\tau \bar{\theta}_{t-1}
+
(1-\tau)\theta_t
$$

where:

- $\bar{\theta}_t$ is the current target encoder parameter.
- $\bar{\theta}_{t-1}$ is the previous target encoder parameter.
- $\theta_t$ is the current context encoder parameter.
- $\tau$ is the momentum coefficient.

For example, if:

$$
\tau = 0.996
$$

then:

$$
\bar{\theta}_t
=
0.996\bar{\theta}_{t-1}
+
0.004\theta_t
$$

This means that the target encoder changes slowly and gradually follows the context encoder.

In I-JEPA, the momentum coefficient starts at approximately:

$$
\tau = 0.996
$$

and gradually increases toward:

$$
\tau = 1.0
$$

during pretraining.

---

### Teacher-Student Interpretation

This mechanism is similar to a **Teacher-Student framework**:

$$
\text{Context Encoder}
\approx
\text{Student}
$$

$$
\text{Target Encoder}
\approx
\text{Slow-Moving Teacher}
$$

However, the target encoder is not a separately pretrained teacher model.

Instead, its parameters are obtained from an exponential moving average of the context encoder parameters:

$$
\boxed{
\bar{\theta}
\leftarrow
\text{EMA}(\bar{\theta},\theta)
}
$$

Therefore, the target encoder provides a relatively stable learning target for the context encoder and predictor during self-supervised pretraining.
